# Finding a presentation idea for a beat

Read in Step 05 (builders), and in Step 07 when the polish replaces the most ordinary moments. This
is a method, not a catalog: no list of forms ships here, and no step uses this page to assign a
form to a segment. Everything in it is a suggestion: take it, combine it, replace it or invent
something better.

The same point ("a knowledge base is a few folders") can be built up layer by layer, filed into a
cabinet, drawn as a map, or acted out. What is on screen is one idea among many that would have
explained it. The look (stage, color, type) is locked; how each beat is *presented* is not.

## For each beat, or group of beats you merge

1. **Ask what the sentence is really about.** Not the words but the thing: what something is made
   of, how it happens, what changes, how two things differ, how big or small it is, an abstract
   claim, a fact that needs proof, how a mess gets ordered. Say it in your own sentence if none
   of these fit.
2. **Ask what the viewer should feel or see by the end of the beat.** The whole picture or a
   detail? A comparison, or one image they will remember? Relief, surprise, recognition?
3. **Look at your references**, if the brief has any, with one question: how does this moment
   perform its idea? Borrow the staging, not the look. Real examples and real interfaces are often
   the strongest idea available.
4. **Try two or three truly different ideas**, each a rough still and one sentence about what the
   viewer sees and feels. Different means a change of metaphor, source material, viewpoint or
   scale, not of color. The first idea is usually the most ordinary; the one you keep should come
   from a different place than the first.
5. **Pick the one that shows rather than decorates.** Could the viewer retell the idea from the
   picture? Say why in a sentence ("staged this way because...") and keep the others in the report.
6. **Do not repeat yourself.** If the last beat used a device, the next one probably should not.
   Alternate big and small, near and far, static and moving, still and acting out. Repeating a
   device on purpose, to build familiarity, is fine; repeating it because it worked once is not.

## When you are stuck

Take the narration's verbs and metaphors literally; ask who performs this best in real life and
borrow how they do it; change the material, the physics, the viewpoint or the scale; let one
element carry meaning and bring it back with a little more each time; subtract where you would
add; borrow another medium's grammar. If a technique is new to you, try it on one still before you
commit.

## After you have built it

Find the three most forgettable moments and give each two or three better ideas
(`review.md`, "Finding better ideas"). Report how every beat was staged and what you used, so the
main agent can see at a glance whether segments are starting to look alike.
